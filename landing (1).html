What the product actually does

Focused on one high-value privacy problem: public invoice amounts.

The product is intentionally narrow for the hackathon. We are not trying to hide everything. We are proving one important commercial fact: a payment fits its contract bounds without leaking the exact number.

01

Bounded settlement

Businesses publish a payment range such as $10,000 to $50,000, then settle a specific invoice without exposing the actual amount.

02

Browser-side proving

Witness generation and proof creation happen off-chain so private inputs never need a backend proving service in the MVP.

03

Selective disclosure

Auditors can later verify amount plus salt against the stored commitment without changing what the chain already reveals.

End-to-end flow

Four steps from invoice registration to private settlement.

This mirrors the development plan and keeps the demo easy to explain in two minutes.

Step 01

Register invoice bounds

The payer records payee plus public `lo_bound` and `hi_bound` on Soroban. No amount is posted.

Step 02

Choose actual payment

The buyer decides the invoice amount privately and generates a random salt for the commitment.

Step 03

Generate Groth16 proof

The browser proves the amount is within bounds and tied to the commitment using Poseidon.

Step 04

Verify before settlement

The contract checks the proof and only then marks the invoice settled, emitting the commitment hash.

Architecture summary

One repo, four build tracks: circuit, prover, contract, frontend.

The implementation plan below matches the repository scaffolding added in this phase.

Circom circuit

Proves `lo_bound ≤ amount ≤ hi_bound` and ties the proof to `Poseidon(amount, salt)` so the settlement is bound to one invoice commitment.

Soroban verifier

Stores invoice metadata, checks bounds against registration, verifies the proof, and emits an event with commitment only.

Frontend demo

Explains the happy path, the rejection path, and the public-versus-private separation judges need to understand fast.
